# FLYER Visual Preset Library

This library contains 40 complete visual recipes in five groups. Recommend one preset automatically from the story. The user may replace it.

## Selection and Mixing

- Choose from story beat, setting, performance scale, light motivation, and material world.
- A preset is a decision system, not a list of decorative adjectives.
- Mix at most two presets. Use a primary at roughly 70 percent and a secondary at roughly 30 percent.
- Name which properties come from each preset.
- Do not combine contradictory exposure, palette, texture, or performance rules without resolving the conflict.
- If the user supplies a film, director, or photographer reference, understand the intent privately and map it to the closest observable preset language. Final prompts should describe the visible decisions rather than depend on a creator name.

# Group A — Narrative and Performance

## A1 — Intimate Observational Realism

- Best for: private decisions, family, friendship, quiet grief, daily life.
- Camera: eye-level medium or close framing, natural perspective, stable observation or restrained handheld breathing.
- Light and color: motivated window or practical light, neutral skin, modest contrast, limited palette.
- Materials and performance: pores, fabric wear, small breath and gaze shifts; environment remains imperfect and lived-in.
- Avoid: glamour posing, heroic low angles, decorative haze, emotional music swell.

## A2 — Restrained Romance

- Best for: attraction, hesitation, reunion, almost-touching, unspoken affection.
- Camera: shared medium frames, selective close-ups only at the emotional turn, gentle lateral relation or slow push.
- Light and color: soft directional light, warm skin against a cooler or neutral environment, delicate highlight rolloff.
- Materials and performance: micro-smiles, delayed eye contact, fingers and shoulders carry tension; tactile fabric and subtle air movement.
- Avoid: beauty-commercial skin, floating petals without cause, excessive bloom, immediate embrace.

## A3 — Domestic Warmth

- Best for: home, food, pets, family rituals, safety with a trace of nostalgia.
- Camera: human-height framing, layered foreground objects, slow subject-led follow or locked table-level view.
- Light and color: warm practical pools balanced by soft daylight; wood, cream, muted yellow, and one restrained accent.
- Materials and performance: steam, worn ceramics, knit texture, crumbs, fingerprints, relaxed body weight.
- Avoid: showroom-clean interiors, orange color cast on everything, over-staged smiles.

## A4 — Urban Loneliness

- Best for: isolation in crowds, late travel, missed connections, anonymous city life.
- Camera: subject small within architecture, compressed depth or distant observation, negative space ahead of the gaze.
- Light and color: mixed practical light, cool ambient fill, selective warm windows or street sources.
- Materials and performance: wet pavement, scratched transit surfaces, tired posture, hands occupied with a small object.
- Avoid: neon on every surface, cyberpunk clutter, random lens flares, melodramatic crying.

## A5 — Psychological Suspense

- Best for: doubt, hidden presence, unreliable perception, slow realization.
- Camera: slightly withheld viewpoint, controlled off-center frames, slow push or static frame with internal movement.
- Light and color: low-key but readable, motivated side or top light, restrained palette with one warning accent.
- Materials and performance: dry mouth, held breath, eye movement before head movement, barely shifting shadows.
- Avoid: dutch angles by default, strobe effects, total blackness, jump-scare sound before visual setup.

## A6 — Investigative Tension

- Best for: searching, following clues, surveillance, procedural discovery.
- Camera: clear geography, purposeful insert shots, shoulder-height tracking, close-ups only for evidence.
- Light and color: practical task lighting, controlled cool neutrals, accurate surface reflections and document legibility.
- Materials and performance: gloved or deliberate hand action, object orientation, eye-to-object eyelines, accumulated desk wear.
- Avoid: random computer graphics, frantic cutting, unexplained blue light, clues that change position.

## A7 — Grounded Kinetic Action

- Best for: pursuit, struggle, escape, impact, athletic movement.
- Camera: subject-led tracking, readable axis, medium-wide body coverage, one strong move per beat.
- Light and color: high readability, motivated hard or broken light, restrained saturation.
- Materials and performance: weight transfer, foot contact, grip, inertia, cloth drag, dust or water responding to motion.
- Avoid: weightless flips, constant shake, speed ramps without purpose, simultaneous orbit and zoom.

## A8 — Epic Solitude

- Best for: a lone figure against scale, departure, sacrifice, aftermath, awe.
- Camera: wide vertical depth, low horizon or towering environment, slow reveal or nearly locked frame.
- Light and color: one dominant environmental source, broad tonal separation, small warm/cool accent on the figure.
- Materials and performance: wind, snow, dust, cloth drag, a restrained final posture rather than victory posing.
- Avoid: armies of effects, over-bright rim light, generic explosions, tiny subject without readable silhouette.

## Recommendation Format

When recommending:

- Primary preset: [ID and name]

- Keep from primary: [camera, performance, light, material, or color decisions]
- Borrow from secondary: [specific limited decisions]
- Why it serves the visible event: [two concise sentences]
- Refuse: [one likely cheap effect or cliché to avoid]
